`calculate_invoice` in `billing/invoice.py` computes an invoice's final amount by threading the order's subtotal through two sequential adjustments. It first calls `apply_tax` from `billing/tax.py`, passing `subtotal` and `order["region"]`, to produce a tax-inclusive amount; internally, `apply_tax` calls `billing/tax_rates.py` to look up the applicable rate for the given region and calls a helper like `get_rate_for_region` to resolve that rate before applying it to the subtotal. The resulting `with_tax` value is then passed to `apply_late_fee` from `billing/late_fee.py`, along with `order["region"]` and `order["days_overdue"]`, to determine whether a late penalty should be added; `apply_late_fee` in turn calls `billing/policy.py` to fetch the region-specific late-fee policy, likely calling a function such as `get_late_fee_policy` to obtain thresholds and penalty rates before conditionally applying a surcharge based on `days_overdue`. `calculate_invoice` returns the final float produced by `apply_late_fee`, meaning the invoice total reflects tax first, then any late-fee surcharge layered on top. Both `apply_tax` and `apply_late_fee` are keyed off `order["region"]`, so `billing/tax_rates.py` and `billing/policy.py` together act as the region-aware configuration sources that `billing/tax.py` and `billing/late_fee.py` calls into.
